# Enterprise consulting poster design system

## Visual grammar

- Use a warm off-white or very light gray field, deep navy structure, muted blue capability areas, and restrained orange emphasis when the enterprise blue-orange preset applies.
- Prefer a management-consulting or editorial business illustration language: professional people, diagnostic boards, process cards, decision paths, and modular tools.
- Avoid cyberpunk, neon glow, robots as protagonists, artificial-intelligence brains, chips, holograms, glossy three-dimensional rendering, generic purple gradients, emoji, and decorative technology spectacle.
- Express technology as a controlled supporting capability inside business rules, review, and operating processes.
- Keep all visible words Chinese unless the source explicitly requires a Latin proper name or acronym.

Use these values as a preset, not universal brand truth. Replace them when authoritative brand tokens exist.

## Composition patterns

Select, reuse, and reshape patterns according to the material and the quality of the available template:

- editorial hero or proposition field;
- journey, milestone, or maturity path;
- split comparison or decision fork;
- system map, ecosystem, architecture, or control boundary;
- diagnosis atlas or case field with unequal emphasis;
- evidence exhibit, annotated example, or proof stack;
- quiet principle, governance, or decision note;
- action area appropriate to the material.

These can be used as individual relationships or as parts of a proven template. Follow `composition-strategy.md` to assess fit rather than requiring novelty.

## Visual hierarchy rules

1. Use one visual focus for each meaningful narrative turn, not every card.
2. Keep scene illustrations fewer and larger than icons.
3. Keep icon detail legible at 40–72 px.
4. Use orange for problems or action, blue for capability, and navy for governance or structural anchors when the preset applies.
5. Keep repeated capabilities visually identical.
6. Concentrate detail where the material is distinctive and let supporting areas breathe.
7. Use unequal widths and asymmetry when importance is unequal.
